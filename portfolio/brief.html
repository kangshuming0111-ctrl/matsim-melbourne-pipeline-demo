Problem & approach

Road options are easy to visualise but difficult to compare reliably. This project connects network and synthetic-demand preparation to MATSim, checks region and input consistency, records execution evidence, and exposes results through a local API and browser interface.

Integration contribution

Python orchestration, durable job history, preflight checks, source/image fingerprints, controlled road changes, aggregate and paired analysis, and interactive replay. Failed or incomplete runs remain visible.

Featured result

Across three seeds, closure increases mean car-trip time by 7.530 seconds. Adding the proposed alternative increases it by a further 0.098 seconds. This experiment does not support a travel-time benefit from that alternative.

Reliability repair

A 90-second observation plus a missing value had produced a 45-second mean. The repaired pipeline reports 90 seconds, one valid observation and one missing value. Shared parsing, metric-specific denominators and regression tests protect downstream comparisons.

SQL & AI evidence

Ten SQL queries, source hashes, paired distributions and a two-page report. Only 6/934 car trips used new-road links per run; same-endpoint shortest free-flow cost stayed unchanged. See SQL evidence and the frozen AI evaluation: 30 development + 20 reserved questions, with a deterministic baseline and retained failures.

A synthetic scenario-analysis prototype: no observed-traffic calibration or proven policy benefit. Transit is teleported. Three seeds do not establish significance or convergence. The local AI assistant routes read-only queries; it does not infer unrestricted causal claims.
